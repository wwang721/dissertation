First and foremost, I would like to express my heartfelt gratitude to my advisor, Professor Brian A. Camley.
The first year of my Ph.D. was tough. Back then, I was taking classes and doing research remotely from China, with day and night reversed, because of the pandemic.
I joined his group in the middle of that year, transferring from hard condensed matter physics into soft matter and biophysics.
I had only a little background in this field, and his patience, encouragement, and clear guidance made the transition not only possible but genuinely enjoyable.
His scientific insight and his dedication to his students have shaped me as a researcher, and I am also grateful that he supported me as a research assistant throughout, which let me focus on my research without worrying about teaching duties.
Most of all, he showed me, as a physicist, how to find the simple principles behind complex biology.
I could not have asked for a better mentor.
